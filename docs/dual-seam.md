# The Dual Seam

## Premise

Three frameworks meet at a single seam:

| Code | Framework | What it describes |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| SEM  | Semantic ontology | What exists, and how meaning and categories are structured |
| GR   | General relativity | Spacetime geometry, gravity, causal structure |
| QM   | Quantum mechanics | States, observables, measurement, superposition |

**The insight:** the seam is *dual*. Each side can be approached from the
other two, and none of them is the privileged starting point. So every
framework must be worked *toward* the other two.

## The three crossings

Each crossing starts from one framework and asks what the other two look like
from inside it.

### 1. SEM → GR + QM
Start from ontology. What kinds of things must exist for spacetime (GR) and
quantum states (QM) to be meaningful? Are they substances, relations,
processes, or information?
- SEM → GR: is spacetime an entity, a relation, or a category?
- SEM → QM: does a superposition denote one thing, many, or a possibility?

### 2. QM → SEM + GR
Start from quantum theory. What do measurement and entanglement require of
meaning and of geometry?
- QM → SEM: does the observer/measurement split force an ontological category?
- QM → GR: can geometry emerge from entanglement structure?

### 3. GR → SEM + QM
Start from geometry. What do relativity and causal structure demand of the
other two?
- GR → SEM: does background independence make all ontology relational?
- GR → QM: what does "a state at a time" mean without a global time?

## Duality check

Because the seam is dual, each opposite pair should agree. Where they
don't, that's where the seam actually is.

| Pair | Forward | Reverse | Agree? | Notes |
|------|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| SEM ↔ GR | SEM → GR | GR → SEM | ? | |
| SEM ↔ QM | SEM → QM | QM → SEM | ? | |
| GR ↔ QM  | GR → QM  | QM → GR  | ? | |

## Open questions
- What exactly is "dual" here: a symmetry, a formal duality (applied twice
  returns the original), or two distinct interfaces?
- Is the seam one object seen three ways, or three pairwise seams?
